How it works

One closed loop, from sample to report

The detection result decides whether to filter again, so filtering follows the count, not a fixed schedule.

Sample preparation

Input

A salt sample is weighed and registered with an ID, type and date so every result can be traced back to its batch.

Dissolution

Input

The salt dissolves in water, leaving the plastic particles suspended so they can be caught and imaged.

Filtration pass

Removal

The solution runs through the filter. This is where microplastic removal happens.

Automated detection

Detection

The residue and filtrate are imaged. The software counts suspected particles and sorts them into fibers, fragments and beads.

Efficiency check

Decision

The remaining count is compared against the acceptance criterion of 15 particles or fewer. Below it, the batch moves on. Above it, the loop decides what happens next.

Repeat or manual review

Safeguard

Over the limit with fewer than 3 passes used: filter again. After 3 passes and still over the limit: the batch is flagged for a human reviewer.

Recovery

Recovery

The purified filtrate is recrystallized into usable salt instead of discarding the batch.

Output and report

Output

The batch gets purified salt and a compliance report with before-and-after counts, removal efficiency and filtration history.

↻

Below limit, under 3 passes: repeat filtering.
The loop only ends when the batch passes or a reviewer takes over.

Decision rules
